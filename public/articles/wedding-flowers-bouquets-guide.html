Wedding Flowers & Bouquets Guide

How to choose wedding flowers on budget, from bouquet styles to seasonal blooms and working with a florist.

The short version

  • Flowers and venue florals typically cost £500–£2,000, depending on season, how much of the venue is dressed and floral variety.
  • In-season, locally grown flowers cost noticeably less than imported or out-of-season blooms — the single biggest lever on the bill.
  • Book a florist consultation 9–12 months out, especially for a peak season (May–September) Saturday wedding.
  • Repurposing ceremony flowers for the reception — moving aisle arrangements to the top table, for example — is one of the simplest ways to cut costs without cutting scale.

Choosing Your Bouquet Style

The bridal bouquet is usually the single most-photographed floral arrangement of the day, so it's worth choosing a style before pricing anything else — it sets the tone for buttonholes, bridesmaid bouquets and often the venue flowers too.

  • Round or posy: a classic, densely packed dome shape. Works with almost any dress and is the easiest style for a florist to price consistently.
  • Cascading: flowers trail below the hand, dramatic and traditional. Uses more stems, so it's typically the most expensive style.
  • Hand-tied: a looser, natural-looking gathered bunch with visible stems. Popular for rustic, barn and outdoor weddings.
  • Single-stem or minimalist: one statement bloom or a very small posy. A genuinely low-cost option that still photographs well.
  • Pressed or dried: everlasting flowers that can be ordered weeks ahead and kept afterwards. Removes the same-week delivery pressure entirely.

Bring photos, not just words, to your first florist consultation. "Romantic" and "wild" mean different things to different florists — a Pinterest board or saved Instagram posts close that gap fast.

Seasonal Flowers & Costs

Choosing blooms that are naturally in season for your wedding month is the single biggest way to control the floral budget — out-of-season and imported flowers carry a real premium.

As a guide, a bridal bouquet typically costs £80–£250, bridesmaid bouquets £40–£100 each, and buttonholes £8–£15 per person — before venue and ceremony flowers are added. For the fuller picture of where flowers sit against every other wedding cost, see EventFlow's UK Wedding Cost Guide 2026.

Ceremony & Reception Flowers

Beyond the bouquets, most floral budgets are split between the ceremony space and the reception room — and there's a well-worn trick for making one set of flowers work twice.

Ceremony

  • Aisle arrangements or pew-end posies, priced per pair or per metre for a runner
  • An arch, arbour or altar arrangement — often the most expensive single item, and a strong photo backdrop
  • A single statement urn or pedestal arrangement for a registry office or small ceremony room

Reception

  • Table centrepieces, priced per table — tall/statement arrangements cost more than low, cluster-style ones
  • Top table or sweetheart table flowers, usually more elaborate than the guest tables
  • Cake flowers, bar and signage florals as smaller finishing touches

The reuse trick: ask your florist to design ceremony arrangements so they can be physically moved — aisle posies become table centres, the arch flowers move behind the top table. It's the easiest way to get two rooms' worth of impact from one set of flowers.

Working With a Florist

A good florist consultation covers more than which flowers you like — it should also cover logistics that are easy to forget until the week of the wedding.

  • Timeline: book 9–12 months ahead for a peak-season Saturday; 4–6 months is usually enough outside peak season.
  • Budget honesty: give a real number early. A florist can design to almost any budget, but only if they know it upfront rather than after quoting a wishlist.
  • Delivery and setup: confirm who sets up the ceremony and reception flowers, at what time, and whether they're collected afterwards or left for guests to take home.
  • Contract and deposit: flowers are perishable and can't be resold if you cancel, so check cancellation terms carefully — see EventFlow's event contracts and deposits guide.
  • Alternatives to fresh flowers: silk or dried arrangements can be bought second-hand and reused — worth a look at EventFlow's second-hand wedding items guide — while a stylist can extend a smaller floral budget with candles, foliage and fabric. Common questions

What's the average cost of wedding flowers in the UK?

Most UK couples spend £500 to £2,000 on flowers and venue florals combined, depending on the season, how much of the venue is dressed and how many arrangements are needed. A bridal bouquet alone typically costs £80 to £250, with buttonholes and bridesmaid bouquets priced per stem on top.

What flowers are in season for a UK wedding?

Spring brings tulips, ranunculus and daffodils; early summer adds peonies and sweet peas; high summer through autumn is best for dahlias, roses and chrysanthemums; winter relies more on evergreen foliage, amaryllis and anemones. Choosing blooms that are naturally in season for your wedding month keeps costs down and avoids paying a premium for imported stock.

Can I do my own wedding flowers?

Yes — DIY flowers are common for smaller weddings and can cut costs significantly, but they take real time: a wholesale flower order, conditioning the stems, and arranging everything within a day or two of the wedding. Many couples compromise by asking a florist for just the bridal bouquet and doing simpler table arrangements themselves.
