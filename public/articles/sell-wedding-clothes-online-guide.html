How to Sell Wedding Clothes Online

Prepare, price and list a preloved wedding dress or bridal outfit with the details UK buyers need to make a confident decision.

The short version

  • Clean and inspect each garment before you list it. Show any marks, wear or missing details plainly.
  • Give actual garment measurements, label size, your height and heel height, plus every alteration you know about.
  • Use bright, unfiltered photos of the front, back, labels, details and any flaws. An honest listing saves time for both sides.
  • Compare similar second hand wedding dresses by designer, condition, size and age before choosing an asking price.
  • Agree on payment, delivery or collection, and returns before handing over an item. Keep a record of the listing and messages.

Prepare Wedding Clothes Before You List Them

A wedding dress, suit or bridesmaid outfit may have been worn for only a day, but a buyer still needs a clear account of its condition. Start by laying the item out in daylight and looking over the hem, underarms, seams, fastenings, lining and areas that touched make-up or fake tan. Check the detachable pieces too: belts, straps, overskirts, veils and garment bags are easy to overlook.

Decide whether professional cleaning is worthwhile for the fabric and the price you expect to achieve. Follow the care label and ask a specialist about delicate embellishment, silk or structured bridal gowns. Keep the cleaning receipt if you have one, but describe what you can actually see after cleaning. “Professionally cleaned” does not mean “flawless.”

Photograph any remaining marks, loose beads, pulled threads or repairs. If the outfit has been stored for a while, take it out of its bag to check for creases, discolouration and odour. A buyer of pre loved wedding dresses will value accurate detail more than a vague “like new” claim.

What else can you sell?

Alongside wedding dresses, consider suits, waistcoats, ties, shoes, hair accessories, veils and bridesmaid dresses. List each separately when sizes or conditions differ, and say exactly which pieces are included. For décor and other items, see our guide to buying and selling second hand wedding items.

Give Measurements and Alteration Details

Bridal sizing varies between designers and a dress altered for one person may no longer match its label. Measure the garment laid flat without stretching it. For a dress, record the bust, waist, hip, shoulder-to-hem and waist-to-hem measurements in centimetres; explain where you took each measurement. Add the labelled size and your approximate measurements at the time of wear only as context, not as a substitute for garment measurements.

State your height and the height of your wedding shoes if the hem was tailored. Mention any shortening, let-outs, taken-in seams, added cups, bustle, changed neckline or removed straps. If you do not know whether the alterations can be reversed, say so. The buyer should ask a tailor before counting on extra seam allowance.

For a suit, include jacket chest and length, sleeve length, trouser waist and inside leg. For shoes, give labelled UK size and note whether the soles show wear. If you are buying an outfit as well, our wedding attire guide helps with outfit planning.

Photograph the Outfit and Write the Listing

Good photos answer the questions a buyer would ask in person. Use daylight, a plain background and a clean lens. Show a full front and back view, then close-ups of the fabric, fastening, designer and size labels, train or hem, and accessories. Include a clear image of each mark or repair. Wedding-day photos can show the silhouette, but add current photos so buyers can judge its condition today.

A strong listing names the garment and designer, style, colour, fabric if known, label size, actual measurements, alterations, condition, cleaning history and what is included. Say whether it can be tried on or collected, and how you plan to pack it if posting. Keep the title readable: “Designer A-line ivory wedding dress, UK 12 label, altered hem” is more useful than a string of repeated keywords.

Example listing: “Ivory crepe wedding dress by [designer], label size 12. Worn once, then cleaned. Bust 88 cm, waist 70 cm, hip 98 cm; shoulder to hem 145 cm. Hem shortened for 168 cm height with 5 cm heels. Small mark inside lining shown in photo 8. Detachable sleeves and garment bag included. Collection in Cardiff or insured postage by agreement.” Replace every placeholder and measurement with your own verified details.

Keep your original purchase information if available, but do not publish order numbers or personal information in listing photos. Use one consistent description across any sites where you advertise; remove other listings promptly after a sale.

Price Preloved Wedding Dresses Realistically

The price you paid is a starting point, not a guarantee of what a second hand bridal gown will sell for. Search for comparable listings by designer, model, size, condition and age. Asking prices show what sellers hope to receive; completed sales, where available, are a better signal of what buyers have paid. Account for cleaning, alterations, missing pieces, postage or platform fees before settling on your minimum acceptable amount.

Newer styles in sought-after sizes may attract more interest, but fit is personal and an altered hem can narrow the pool of buyers. An unusually high price may leave the listing sitting for months. Leave modest room for negotiation if you wish, and state whether the price includes shipping. You can adjust it after seeing genuine enquiries, rather than making a large cut immediately.

For 2nd hand wedding dresses UK shoppers can compare, transparency matters: a realistic price alongside measurements and flaw photos gives buyers a reason to enquire. Avoid claiming an outfit is “rare” or “unworn” unless you can support it.

Where to Sell Wedding Clothes Online in the UK

Choose a place where buyers expect to browse bridal clothing and where the listing format lets you share measurements and condition. A wedding-specific marketplace can put your item in front of couples already planning their day. Broader second hand fashion sites and local groups may reach more people, but enquiries can vary in relevance. Check the current listing, payment and postage terms of any platform before committing.

You can browse the EventFlow Marketplace for wedding items and explore whether it suits your listing. If you advertise in more than one place, keep stock and pricing consistent. For a high-value gown, collection or an agreed try-on may answer fit questions; set clear boundaries about the location, time and who will be present. A local bridal consignment shop is another option if you prefer help with display and enquiries; confirm its commission and collection terms in writing.

People may search for “preloved wedding dresses,” “second hand wedding dresses” or “2nd hand wedding dresses.” Use a natural description that reflects what you actually sell. A precise designer, silhouette, size and location are often more helpful than repeating generic terms.

Handle Buyer Enquiries and the Handover

Answer fit and condition questions with fresh measurements or photos when needed. Never promise that a gown will fit exactly: a buyer should compare measurements and allow for tailoring. If someone asks to reserve it, agree on a clear deadline and whether any payment is involved. Record the final price, included items and handover arrangements in your messages.

For collection, choose a sensible meeting place and let someone know your plans. For postage, agree on packing, tracking, insurance and who pays before dispatch. Protect delicate fabric with appropriate packaging and keep proof of posting. Use the payment process offered by your chosen platform where applicable, and be wary of requests to move a transaction into an unfamiliar link or share a verification code.

Be explicit about any return arrangement and follow the platform’s rules. Keep photos of the garment and packaging at handover. Once the sale is complete, mark the listing sold so another buyer does not waste time. For more general transaction advice, read our second hand wedding items safety tips.

Checklist: Ready to Sell Your Bridal Outfit?

  • Inspect the item in daylight and photograph every flaw.
  • Clean it appropriately and confirm exactly what is included.
  • Measure the garment and document all alterations you know about.
  • Take current front, back, detail, label and condition photos.
  • Compare similar second hand bridal gowns and set an asking price.
  • Choose a suitable listing platform and explain collection or delivery.
  • Confirm payment and handover terms with the buyer in writing.

Selling wedding clothes gives a thoughtfully chosen outfit another occasion to be worn. A complete, honest listing makes that handover easier and helps the next couple plan with confidence.
