Wedding DIY Projects Guide

Which wedding DIY projects are worth the time, from decor and favours to what is better left to the professionals, with realistic time estimates for each one.

The short version

  • The best DIY projects can be made well ahead of time and don't need to be perfect on the day itself — signage and favours, not the cake.
  • Budget real hours, not just materials cost — a project priced at "just the cost of ribbon" often takes 15+ hours across a full guest list.
  • Anything guests will eat, or that's time-sensitive like flowers, is usually cheaper and less risky to buy in.
  • A DIY squad of friends and family, briefed properly with a clear template, gets more done in less stress than doing it solo.

What's Worth DIYing

The projects that work best as DIY share one thing in common: they can be made well ahead of the wedding and don't depend on being perfect on the day itself.

  • Signage: a welcome sign, table numbers or a seating chart — low risk, and easy to correct mistakes with no time pressure.
  • Favours: simple, bulk-buyable components assembled in advance travel and store well until the day.
  • Stationery add-ons: place cards and small paper details are a manageable DIY extension of printed invitations — see EventFlow's wedding stationery guide for the printed side.
  • Simple decor: table runners, jam-jar candles and similar low-complexity styling touches.

What to Leave to the Professionals

  • Flowers: perishable and genuinely time-pressured in the final days — see EventFlow's Wedding Flowers & Bouquets Guide for a realistic look at what DIY flowers actually involve.
  • The cake: needs real baking skill, time and food-safety awareness for a large group — see the Wedding Cake Guide.
  • Catering: food safety and volume both scale badly for a DIY approach once guest numbers pass a small gathering.
  • Anything needing specialist equipment or hire: lighting rigs, marquees and similar setups are usually cheaper and safer through a supplier than improvised.

Popular Projects & Time Estimates

Materials cost is rarely the real budget for a DIY project — time is, and it's easy to underestimate for a full guest list.

Typical time commitment for common DIY projects
ProjectTypical timeNotes
Welcome sign2–4 hoursOne-off item, low risk of running out of time
Table numbers / place cards (80 guests)4–8 hoursScales directly with guest count
Favours (80 guests)6–10 hoursMostly assembly time once materials are sourced
Photo booth backdrop3–5 hoursOne-off, but needs transport and setup time on the day too

For guest-count-scaling projects especially, budget the hours before committing — a project that looked cheap on paper can become the most stressful part of the run-up if the guest list grows after you've started.

Getting Help Without Losing Control

  • Recruit early: ask for help as soon as a project is decided, not when the deadline is already close.
  • Give a clear brief: a template, a sample piece or clear written instructions get more consistent results than a verbal description.
  • Batch the work: one or two dedicated "craft evenings" with helpers get more done than scattered solo sessions.
  • Set a hard deadline: finish at least 2 weeks before the wedding, not wedding week — leave room for something to go wrong without it becoming urgent.
  • Know when to stop: a project that's causing real stress isn't saving money if you end up paying someone to finish or fix it anyway.

Common questions

What wedding DIY projects are actually worth doing?

The best DIY projects are ones that can be made well ahead of time and don't rely on being perfect on the day itself — signage, table numbers, simple decor and favours are all good candidates. Anything time-sensitive, like flowers, or safety-critical, like the cake, is usually better left to a professional.

Should I DIY my wedding flowers?

It's possible for a smaller wedding but takes real time: a wholesale flower order, conditioning the stems and arranging everything within a day or two of the wedding. Many couples compromise by asking a florist for just the bridal bouquet and doing simpler table arrangements themselves — see EventFlow's Wedding Flowers & Bouquets Guide for more detail.

How far in advance should DIY wedding projects be finished?

Aim to finish DIY projects at least 2 weeks before the wedding, not wedding week itself. Set a hard deadline when you start each project, and recruit help early with a clear brief so the work can be batched into one or two sessions rather than done piecemeal.
