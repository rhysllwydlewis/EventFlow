Getting Married at Gretna Green

Why couples marry at Gretna Green, the residency rules that apply, and how to book a ceremony there.

The short version

  • Gretna Green is the first village in Scotland over the border from England, famous since the 18th century as a destination for couples to marry quickly under Scots law.
  • You don't need to live in Scotland, or have any connection to it, to marry there — the rules apply to visitors just as they do to residents.
  • You must submit a marriage notice to the local district registrar between 3 months and 29 days before your ceremony date.
  • The Old Blacksmith's Shop is the best-known venue, but Gretna Green has grown into a full wedding destination with several licensed venues.

Why Gretna Green?

Gretna Green is a small village just across the Scottish border from England, and it has been a wedding destination since the 18th century. Scots marriage law historically allowed couples to marry younger, and without parental consent, than the law in England — so couples travelled north to marry quickly, often at the village's Old Blacksmith's Shop, where the local blacksmith would perform an "anvil" wedding as the first legal adult they met on Scottish soil.

That history is why Gretna Green is still closely associated with elopement and quick, romantic weddings today, even though most couples who marry there now do so for the setting and tradition rather than to avoid a legal restriction back home.

Do you need to live in Scotland to marry there?

No. Scots marriage law has no residency requirement — you can marry in Scotland as a visitor, with no connection to the country beyond choosing to marry there. What you do need is to submit a marriage notice to the district registrar covering the area where you'll marry, between 3 months and 29 days before your ceremony date, along with the standard documents (proof of identity, address and, where relevant, evidence any previous marriage or civil partnership has legally ended).

Book the registrar and venue early. The 29-day minimum notice period is a legal floor, not a booking guarantee — popular Gretna Green venues and dates get booked well over a year ahead.

How to book a wedding at Gretna Green

  1. Pick your venue. The Old Blacksmith's Shop is the most famous, but Gretna Green now has several licensed wedding venues nearby, from historic to modern.
  2. Confirm your ceremony type — civil, religious or belief-based — since each has slightly different requirements for who can conduct it.
  3. Submit your marriage notice to the district registrar for the area, no earlier than 3 months and no later than 29 days before the date.
  4. Book your suppliers — photographer, catering, flowers and transport — as you would for any wedding, factoring in that many guests will be travelling.
  5. Bring the right documents on the day, including photo ID and your marriage schedule once it's issued.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Confirm the current process and documents with the registrar for the district you'll marry in before you book anything.

Common questions

Why do people get married at Gretna Green?

Historically, couples eloped to Gretna Green because Scots law let them marry younger, and without parental consent, than English law allowed. It's the first village over the Scottish border, so it became the obvious destination. Today most couples choose it for the setting and tradition rather than to get around a legal restriction.

Do you have to live in Scotland to marry at Gretna Green?

No. There's no residency requirement to marry in Scotland — you can marry there as a visitor. You do need to submit a marriage notice to the local district registrar between 3 months and 29 days before your ceremony.

How far in advance should I book a Gretna Green wedding?

Much further ahead than the legal 29-day notice minimum suggests. Popular venues and dates, especially at well-known sites like the Old Blacksmith's Shop, are often booked well over a year in advance.
