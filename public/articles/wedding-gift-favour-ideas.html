Wedding Gift & Favour Ideas

Affordable, memorable wedding favour ideas for guests, plus gift ideas for the couple, organised by budget.

The short version

  • Wedding favours are a small thank-you for guests; wedding and engagement gifts are for the couple — the two are easily confused but call for very different ideas.
  • Inexpensive favours (under £1–3 per guest) are consistently just as well received as expensive ones — guests remember the gesture, not the price.
  • Edible and consumable favours (sweets, mini bottles, seed packets) avoid the "what do I do with this" problem of ornamental keepsakes.
  • For the couple's own gift, a contribution towards the honeymoon or a shared experience is increasingly preferred over a traditional homeware gift.

Wedding Favours by Budget

A wedding favour is a small token given to each guest as a thank-you for attending — distinct from a gift given to the couple. Guests generally remember the gesture rather than the price, so an inexpensive favour is rarely a false economy.

Under £1 per guest

  • Personalised mini chocolate bars or sweets in a small organza bag
  • Seed packets, printed with the couple's names and wedding date
  • A single mini bottle of bubbles for the dance floor
  • A printed thank-you note or luggage tag place card that doubles as the favour

£1–£3 per guest

  • Mini candles or a small jar of local honey/jam
  • A miniature bottle of wine, gin or prosecco
  • A small succulent or potted plant
  • A personalised keyring, coaster or bottle opener

Splurge favours

  • A local, higher-end food item (fudge, chutney, a small hamper item) sourced from a wedding supplier
  • A charitable donation made in each guest's honour, with a small card explaining the cause
  • A disposable camera on each table, doubling as a favour and a candid photo source
  • A late-night treat station (doughnuts, a sweet cart) that guests take a portion of home

Edible or consumable favours are the safest choice at any budget — they avoid the "what do I do with this ornament" problem that unused favours often create.

Gift Ideas for the Couple

Wedding and engagement gifts are a separate category from favours — these are for the couple themselves, usually chosen by guests from a gift list or their own judgement.

Traditional gifts

  • Homeware: cookware, bedding, glassware — often chosen from a wedding gift list at a department store
  • Personalised keepsakes: engraved items, a custom illustration of the venue, a framed photo
  • Cash or vouchers, given discreetly in a card

Increasingly popular alternatives

  • A contribution towards the honeymoon, via a honeymoon fund on the couple's wedding website
  • Experience gifts — tickets, a cookery class, a subscription the couple will use together
  • A group gift from several guests towards one larger item the couple has specifically requested

If you're the couple and want to guide guests without asking directly, EventFlow's wedding website guide covers setting up a gift list or honeymoon fund guests can contribute to online.

Common questions

What are good cheap wedding favour ideas?

Edible or consumable favours work best at low budgets: personalised sweets or chocolates, seed packets, a mini bottle of bubbles, or a jar of local honey or jam. Guests remember the gesture, not the price.

What's the difference between a wedding favour and a wedding gift?

A wedding favour is a small thank-you the couple gives to each guest. A wedding or engagement gift goes the other way — it's given by guests to the couple, usually chosen from a gift list or their own judgement.

Is it acceptable to ask for a honeymoon fund instead of gifts?

Yes — honeymoon funds and experience-based group gifts are now widely accepted in the UK. Most couples set this up through their wedding website rather than asking directly on the invitation itself.
