RSVP Management Guide: Replies, Dietary Needs and Plus-Ones

RSVPs are not just a yes-or-no list. They drive catering, seating, supplier numbers, accessibility arrangements and your final cost control.

📚 7 min read • Updated June 2026

In this guide
  1. Ask the right questions
  2. Keep the list clean
  3. Chase politely
  4. Lock numbers before deadlines

Ask the right questions first time

Collect all the details you will need before suppliers start asking for final numbers. At a minimum, ask whether each guest is attending, whether they have a dietary requirement, whether they need accessibility support and whether any approved plus-one or child place is being used.

Keep questions short. Guests are more likely to respond quickly when the form is clear and mobile friendly.

Keep the guest list clean

A clean RSVP list has one record per invited person, consistent names and clear status labels. Avoid keeping one list in messages, another in a spreadsheet and another in your head. That is how duplicates, missed meals and awkward table-plan issues happen.

  • Use one source of truth.
  • Mark invited, accepted, declined and chasing separately.
  • Keep notes short and factual.
  • Review the list before each supplier deadline.

Chase without making it awkward

Set the RSVP deadline earlier than your supplier deadline. That gives you time to chase calmly, not urgently. A short, friendly message usually works best: remind them why you need the response and give a clear deadline.

Lock numbers before supplier deadlines

Caterers, venues, stationers and hire companies all work from final numbers. Once those figures are submitted, changes can cost money. Before locking numbers, check plus-ones, children, allergies, evening guests and supplier meals.
