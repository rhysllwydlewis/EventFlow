Hen Do & Stag Do Planning Guide

How to plan a hen do or stag do, including budget, who pays, timing and ideas for every style.

The short version

  • Most UK hen dos and stag dos happen 1–6 months before the wedding — never in the final couple of weeks.

When to Plan It

Most UK hen dos and stag dos take place one to six months before the wedding — early enough that recovery time isn't an issue, late enough that it still feels connected to the countdown.

  • Avoid the final 2–4 weeks: a heavy weekend, a late night or a minor injury this close to the wedding is a genuine risk not worth taking.
  • Weekday vs weekend: a weekday break is often cheaper and less crowded, but a weekend usually gets a higher guest turnout — check who's essential before fixing a date.
  • Book 2–4 months ahead: for activities, accommodation and group rates, particularly for a summer weekend or a popular UK city break.
  • Check the wedding planning timeline: see EventFlow's event planning checklist for where a hen or stag do typically sits against other wedding milestones.

Budget & Who Pays

Cost is consistently the biggest source of friction around hen and stag dos — agreeing it early avoids awkward conversations later.

  • Typical UK cost: £150–£400+ per person, depending on activity, travel and whether it's a single night or a full weekend away.
  • Who pays what: the most common approach is that each guest covers their own share, while the organiser (maid/matron of honour or best man) often covers the guest of honour's costs, sometimes topped up by a small group contribution.
  • Set a cap, not just a plan: agree a maximum per-person spend before booking anything, so nobody finds out the total cost after they've already said yes.
  • Use a shared tracker: a group payment app or simple spreadsheet keeps deposits, instalments and who's paid what visible to everyone, and avoids one person fronting the whole cost.

If the guest list includes people on noticeably different budgets, offer optional add-ons (a spa treatment, an extra excursion) rather than one expensive package everyone's expected to join.

Ideas by Style

Not every hen do or stag do needs to be a big blow-out — matching the style to the guest of honour matters more than matching a stereotype.

Relaxed

  • A spa day or weekend
  • A cocktail or wine-tasting masterclass
  • A home-based night in with food, games and no set schedule

Active

  • Paintballing, axe throwing or an escape room
  • Watersports — paddleboarding, kayaking or a boat day
  • A countryside walk or hike followed by a pub lunch

City breaks and abroad

  • Classic UK city weekends — Brighton, Newcastle, Edinburgh, Cardiff
  • Short-haul European breaks — Amsterdam, Barcelona, Dublin, Prague
  • Check passport validity and any visa requirements early if travelling abroad

Traditional and low-key

  • A classic night out at home with close friends and family
  • A themed dinner party or afternoon tea
  • A single low-cost day out for groups where a full weekend away isn't realistic for everyone

Etiquette & Invites

  • Guest list: the hen or stag guest list doesn't need to match the wedding guest list exactly — it's usually close friends and immediate family, decided with the guest of honour's input.
  • Inclusive planning: ask about dietary needs, access requirements and comfort with alcohol or the planned activities before finalising the plan, not after.
  • Communication: a single group chat for logistics, payments and updates keeps everyone informed without excluding anyone who joins the plan later.
  • Thanking the organiser: planning a hen or stag do is genuine, unpaid work — a small thank-you gesture from the group is a common and appreciated courtesy.

For managing the wider guest list and RSVPs around the wedding itself, see EventFlow's guest list management guide.

Common questions

Who pays for a hen do or stag do?

There's no fixed rule, but the most common UK approach is that each guest covers their own share of activities, food and accommodation, while the organiser — usually the maid or matron of honour, or the best man — often covers the guest of honour's costs, sometimes with a small contribution from the group.

How far in advance should you book a hen do or stag do?

Most UK hen dos and stag dos happen 1 to 6 months before the wedding, and are booked 2 to 4 months ahead to secure activities, accommodation and better group rates. Avoid the final 2 to 4 weeks before the wedding, when there's less time to recover from a big weekend or an unexpected injury.

Should the couple be involved in planning their own hen or stag do?

Not usually in the details — the surprise element is part of the tradition — but the organiser should still check hard no's in advance: a country the guest of honour won't fly to, an activity they physically can't or won't do, or a date that clashes with something else in the wedding run-up.
