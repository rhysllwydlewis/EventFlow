How to Build Your Wedding Website on EventFlow

A good wedding website should stop repeat questions, collect the details you need and give guests one trusted place to check information.

📚 8 min read • Updated June 2026

In this guide
  1. What to include
  2. Reducing guest questions
  3. Privacy and polish
  4. Connect it to EventFlow

Start with what guests actually need

Your website should answer the practical questions guests ask in the final few weeks: where to go, when to arrive, what to wear, how to RSVP and who to contact if something changes.

  • Venue name, address, parking and accessibility notes.
  • Ceremony, reception and evening timings.
  • Dress code, children policy and plus-one wording.
  • Accommodation, taxi and local travel information.
  • RSVP deadline and dietary requirement instructions.

Use the website to reduce repeat questions

Think of the website as your public FAQ. If you are being asked the same question twice, add the answer. Keep wording clear and practical rather than overly formal.

A strong wedding website helps protect your time because guests can self-serve the basics without messaging you during the busiest part of planning.

Keep it polished and private

Only publish information guests need. Avoid adding private supplier contracts, personal phone numbers or any details you would not want forwarded. Use short sections, headings and plain language so guests can find information quickly on a phone.

Connect it back to your EventFlow workflow

Use your EventFlow plan as the source of truth. Match your guest list, RSVP wording and timeline to the information on the website, then review the page whenever a supplier deadline changes.
