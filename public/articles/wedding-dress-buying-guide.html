Wedding Dress Buying Guide

How to choose a wedding dress: silhouettes, fabrics, sizing, ordering timeline, budgeting and where to buy in the UK.

The short version

  • Order a made-to-order dress 9–12 months ahead; off-the-peg, sample-sale or made-to-measure options can be ready in weeks if you're short on time.
  • Bridal sizing commonly runs smaller than high-street sizing — get measured by the boutique rather than assuming your usual size.
  • Most UK brides spend somewhere in the £1,000–£1,500 range before alterations, though this varies enormously by designer and fabric.
  • Second-hand, sample-sale and rental dresses are all established, lower-cost routes in the UK — not just a budget compromise.

Choosing a Silhouette & Fabric

The silhouette is the single biggest factor in how a dress looks and moves, and it's worth trying on several shapes before deciding — the style you picture in your head isn't always the one that suits you best in person.

Silhouettes

  • A-line: fitted through the bodice, gradually flaring from the waist. Flattering on most body shapes and a safe, versatile starting point.
  • Ball gown: a fitted bodice with a dramatically full skirt — classic and formal, needs more room to move and sit in.
  • Mermaid/fishtail: fitted through the body and flaring at or below the knee. Dramatic and figure-hugging, but can restrict walking stride.
  • Sheath/column: a straight, slim cut from top to bottom. Minimalist and modern, often chosen for smaller or more relaxed weddings.
  • Empire line: a raised waistline just below the bust, falling loosely from there. Comfortable and forgiving, a common choice for pregnancy or an informal venue.

Fabric

Satin holds structure and photographs well; lace adds texture and detail and is often layered over a plainer underlay; tulle gives volume for a fuller skirt; chiffon and crepe drape softly and suit a relaxed or outdoor wedding; organza sits between tulle and satin for a lighter structured look. Fabric weight also affects comfort — a heavier structured dress can be warm for a summer wedding or a marquee.

Sizing & Ordering Timeline

Wedding dress sizing and lead times catch a lot of brides out, mainly because bridal sizing doesn't match everyday clothing sizes.

Typical ordering timeline
RouteTypical lead timeNotes
Made-to-order from a boutique6–12 monthsLonger for popular designers or a peak-season wedding
Made-to-measure3–6 monthsBuilt to your exact measurements rather than a standard size chart
Off-the-peg / in stockDays to weeksLimited to what the boutique has on the rail in your size
Sample sale or second-handImmediateOne-off pieces — see EventFlow's second-hand wedding items guide

Book alterations separately once the dress arrives — most boutiques recommend 2 to 3 fittings, with the final one 2 to 4 weeks before the wedding so it accounts for any last-minute change in fit.

Costs & Budgeting

UK wedding dress spend varies enormously by designer, fabric and where you buy, but most surveys of UK brides put the typical figure somewhere in the £1,000–£1,500 range before alterations. Alterations commonly add another £100–£300 depending on how much work the dress needs — a bigger factor than many brides budget for upfront. For how dress spend compares to the rest of a wedding budget, see the UK Wedding Cost Guide 2026.

Other costs to plan for beyond the dress itself: a petticoat or underskirt, a veil or headpiece, shoes, and a garment bag for travel or storage — these are often quoted separately from the dress price.

Where to Buy

  • Bridal boutiques: appointment-based, with staff to guide sizing and style, though alterations are often quoted separately from the dress price.
  • Department store and high-street bridal ranges: generally faster turnaround and lower price points than a specialist boutique.
  • Made-to-measure online: built to your own measurements rather than a standard size, often at a lower price than a boutique equivalent — check the maker's returns policy carefully before ordering, since fit issues can be harder to resolve remotely.
  • Sample sales and pre-loved: boutiques regularly sell past-season sample dresses at a discount, and a growing second-hand market offers worn-once dresses — see EventFlow's second-hand wedding items guide for buying and selling safely.
  • Dress rental: a growing option for brides who want a designer look without the full purchase cost, typically for a single wear over a short hire window.

Whatever the dress, see EventFlow's Wedding Attire Guide for how it coordinates with what the rest of the wedding party wears, and the Wedding Rings & Jewellery Guide for jewellery that complements the neckline.

Common questions

How far in advance should I buy my wedding dress?

Order a made-to-order dress 9 to 12 months before the wedding to allow for production, delivery and 2 to 3 fittings. If you're short on time, an off-the-peg sample, a sample-sale dress or a made-to-measure dress from a faster-turnaround designer can be ready in weeks rather than months.

How much does a UK wedding dress cost?

UK wedding dress spend varies enormously by designer, fabric and where you buy, but most surveys of UK brides put the typical figure somewhere in the £1,000 to £1,500 range before alterations. Alterations commonly add another £100 to £300 depending on how much work the dress needs.

Do I need a special size for a wedding dress?

Bridal sizing commonly runs smaller than everyday high-street sizing, and sizing charts vary between designers, so don't assume your usual dress size will match. Get measured by the boutique and order to your measurements rather than the size on the label — that's what alterations are for.
