How to Choose a Wedding Photographer

What to ask a wedding photographer before booking, how much it costs in the UK, and how to compare styles and packages.

The short version

  • Most UK wedding photographers now blend traditional posed shots with documentary-style candid coverage rather than sticking to one style.
  • Full-day coverage commonly falls somewhere in the £1,200–£3,000 range, with albums and a second shooter usually costing extra.
  • Ask to see full galleries from entire real weddings, not just a highlight reel — it's the best way to judge consistency across a whole day.
  • Book 9–12 months ahead, earlier for a popular photographer or a peak season Saturday.

Photography Styles

Knowing the difference between photography styles makes it much easier to judge whether a photographer's portfolio matches what you actually want from your wedding photos.

  • Traditional/classic: posed and directed — formal portraits, family groups and key staged moments (the first kiss, the cutting of the cake).
  • Documentary/reportage: candid, unposed coverage that captures the day as it unfolds, with minimal direction from the photographer.
  • Fine art/editorial: a styled, considered look with careful composition and light — often slower-paced to shoot, well suited to couples who enjoy a more directed portrait session.

Most working UK wedding photographers now offer a blend: key posed shots (group photos, portraits) alongside documentary coverage of the rest of the day, so ask to see how a photographer balances the two rather than assuming a single label tells you everything.

What to Ask Before Booking

  • See full galleries, not just highlights: ask for two or three complete real weddings, not a curated highlight reel — this shows consistency across a whole day, including the less flattering light of a reception.
  • Second shooter: ask whether a second photographer is included, useful for capturing both partners getting ready simultaneously or covering a large guest list.
  • Backup equipment and insurance: confirm they carry backup cameras/cards and public liability insurance — many UK venues now require proof of this before a supplier can work there.
  • Turnaround time: ask how long after the wedding you'll receive edited images and any album, since this varies widely between photographers.
  • Image rights: clarify who owns the copyright and what you're licensed to do with the photos (printing, sharing online, submitting to publications).
  • Contract terms: check the deposit, cancellation and postponement terms before booking — see EventFlow's event contracts and deposits guide for what to look for across any supplier contract.

Costs & Packages

UK wedding photography costs vary widely by region, hours of coverage and experience, but full-day coverage commonly falls somewhere in the £1,200–£3,000 range. Half-day or elopement packages cost less; a second shooter, engagement shoot, printed albums and same-day editing are usually optional add-ons rather than included as standard. For how photography spend compares to the rest of a wedding budget, see the UK Wedding Cost Guide 2026.

Compare quotes on what's actually included — hours of coverage, number of edited images delivered, and whether travel is charged separately — rather than the headline price alone. Browse EventFlow's photography suppliers to compare local packages.

Booking Timeline

  • 9–12 months before: book a popular photographer, especially for a peak-season (May–September) Saturday.
  • 4–6 months before: book if you haven't already — this is the minimum for most in-demand photographers.
  • 1–2 months before: agree the running order and confirm any specific shots you want, using EventFlow's photography shot list template as a starting point.
  • On the day: share the final timeline with your photographer so they know when key moments (speeches, first dance, cake cutting) are happening.

Common questions

How much does a wedding photographer cost in the UK?

UK wedding photography costs vary widely by region, hours of coverage and experience, but full-day coverage commonly falls somewhere in the £1,200 to £3,000 range. Engagement shoots, printed albums and a second shooter are usually optional add-ons on top of the base package.

What's the difference between traditional and documentary wedding photography?

Traditional photography is posed and directed — group shots, portraits and key moments arranged by the photographer. Documentary (or reportage) photography captures the day candidly as it happens, with little to no posing. Most UK wedding photographers now blend both, covering key posed shots alongside candid coverage.

How far in advance should I book a wedding photographer?

Book a wedding photographer 9 to 12 months ahead, and earlier still for a popular photographer or a peak season (May–September) Saturday. Confirm your shot list and running order with them a few weeks before the wedding.
