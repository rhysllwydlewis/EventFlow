Prenups in the UK: A Complete Guide

What a UK prenuptial agreement is, whether it's legally binding, and how to get one before your wedding.

The short version

  • A prenup is not automatically legally binding in England and Wales, but courts have upheld them since Radmacher v Granatino (2010) when they're entered into fairly.
  • Scotland treats pre-marriage financial agreements differently under Scots law, so get advice specific to where you plan to marry.

  • Sign well before the wedding — commonly at least 28 days beforehand — so neither party can later argue they were pressured.

What is a prenup?

A prenuptial agreement, or prenup, is a written contract two people sign before marrying that sets out how their money, property and other assets would be divided if the marriage later ends. It typically covers what each partner brings into the marriage, jointly acquired property and debts, and sometimes ongoing maintenance.

Couples usually consider one when there's a significant difference in wealth, one partner is bringing a business or inheritance into the marriage, or either has children from a previous relationship whose interests they want to protect.

Is a prenup legally binding in the UK?

In England and Wales, a prenup is not automatically binding — courts retain the final say over financial arrangements on divorce. But since the Supreme Court's 2010 ruling in Radmacher v Granatino, judges will usually uphold a prenup's terms provided both partners entered into it freely, understood what it meant, and it wouldn't be unfair to hold them to it. Scotland's approach to pre-marriage financial agreements differs under Scots family law, so always get advice specific to where you live and plan to marry.

For a prenup to carry real weight, courts generally expect: both partners had independent legal advice, each fully disclosed their finances, it was signed with enough time before the wedding, and its terms don't leave either partner — or any children — in genuine need.

How to get a prenup

  1. Start the conversation early — ideally months before the wedding, not weeks.
  2. Each partner instructs their own solicitor. One solicitor cannot advise both sides, since your interests are opposed.
  3. Disclose your full financial position to each other: assets, debts, income and pensions.
  4. Agree terms together, then have your solicitors draft and review the agreement.
  5. Sign well ahead of the wedding day — commonly at least 28 days beforehand.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. For an agreement that will hold up in your specific circumstances, speak to a family solicitor.

Common questions

What is a prenup?

A prenuptial agreement is a written contract signed before marriage that sets out how a couple's money, property and other assets would be divided if the marriage later ends.

Are prenups legally binding in the UK?

Not automatically — English and Welsh courts still have the final say — but since Radmacher v Granatino (2010) they are usually upheld if both partners took independent legal advice, disclosed their finances fully, and signed well before the wedding.

Do both partners need their own solicitor?

Yes. One solicitor cannot advise both partners, since their interests are opposed — each side needs independent legal advice for the agreement to carry real weight.
