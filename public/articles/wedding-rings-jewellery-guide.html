Wedding Rings & Jewellery Guide

Choosing engagement rings, wedding bands and jewellery for the wedding party, with buying and insurance tips.

The short version

  • There's no fixed rule for engagement ring spend — the "two months' salary" guideline is a De Beers marketing line, not a UK norm.
  • Wedding bands don't need to match the engagement ring or each other — comfort and daily wear matter more than a coordinated set.
  • Insure rings separately, since many standard home contents policies cap single-item claims well below a ring's real value.
  • Buying second-hand or antique rings is an established, lower-cost route in the UK — not just a budget compromise.

Engagement Rings

Choosing an engagement ring usually comes down to two decisions: the stone and the setting. Understanding the basics of each makes it far easier to compare quotes from different jewellers.

The 4 Cs

  • Cut: how well the stone is shaped and faceted — the biggest single factor in how much a diamond sparkles, independent of size.
  • Colour: graded from colourless to noticeably tinted. Near-colourless grades are often visually indistinguishable but cost less.
  • Clarity: internal and surface marks, rated on a scale from flawless downwards. Most are invisible to the naked eye above a certain grade.
  • Carat: the stone's weight, not its size — cut quality affects how large a stone appears more than carat weight alone.

Stone choices

Lab-grown diamonds are chemically identical to mined diamonds and typically cost 30–40% less for the same specification — a fast-growing choice among UK couples. Alternative stones like sapphire, moissanite or a family heirloom stone are also well-established, lower-cost or more personal options.

Ask for independent certification (such as GIA or IGI) on any diamond over a modest value, and ask about provenance — reputable UK jewellers can confirm a diamond is conflict-free under the Kimberley Process.

Wedding Bands

Wedding bands are worn daily for decades, so comfort and durability matter at least as much as how they look on the day.

  • Metal: platinum is the most durable and hypoallergenic but the most expensive; 18ct or 9ct gold is a common mid-range choice; palladium and titanium are lighter, budget-friendly alternatives.
  • Matching or not: bands don't need to match each other or the engagement ring — many couples choose theirs entirely separately based on what's comfortable to wear.
  • Fit: have bands properly sized close to the wedding, since fingers can change size seasonally; most jewellers need 2–6 weeks to size or engrave a ring.
  • Engraving: a date or short message inside the band is a popular, low-cost personal touch — confirm the character limit before ordering.

Jewellery for the Wedding Party

Beyond the rings, jewellery choices extend to everyone in the wedding line-up — and a little coordination goes a long way without needing to match exactly.

The bride

Simpler is usually better: earrings, a delicate necklace or a hair piece that complements the dress neckline rather than competing with it. See EventFlow's Wedding Attire Guide for how jewellery fits with dress style more broadly.

Bridesmaids

A popular option is a "gift and wear on the day" piece — earrings or a bracelet given as a thank-you gift that also becomes part of the outfit, covered further in EventFlow's gift and favour ideas guide.

The groom and groomsmen

Cufflinks, a tie pin or a watch are the main options, and matching cufflinks across the groom's party is a simple, affordable way to tie outfits together without full colour-coordination.

Buying Tips & Insurance

  • Where to buy: independent jewellers often offer more personalisation and better after-sales resizing; high street chains offer convenience and finance options; pre-loved or antique rings from a specialist dealer or marketplace can cost significantly less — see EventFlow's second-hand wedding items guide.
  • Get it valued: a professional valuation (separate from the receipt) is usually required to insure a ring properly and should be updated every few years as prices change.
  • Insure separately: many standard home contents policies cap single-item claims well below a ring's value — check your policy or add specialist jewellery cover. EventFlow's event insurance guide covers wedding-day cover more broadly.
  • Keep the paperwork: receipts, certificates and valuations all matter for insurance claims, resizing warranties and any future resale.

Common questions

How much should I spend on an engagement ring?

There is no fixed rule — the well-known "two months' salary" guideline started as a De Beers marketing campaign, not a financial or cultural norm. UK spend varies enormously; what matters more is agreeing a comfortable budget as a couple (if buying together) and factoring in insurance from day one.

Do wedding bands need to match?

No. Many couples choose bands separately based on comfort, metal preference and daily wear rather than buying a matching set. Some do coordinate metal tone or finish with the engagement ring; others deliberately choose different styles for each partner.

Should I insure my wedding rings?

It's worth checking, since many standard home contents policies cap single-item claims well below a ring's value. Options include adding named-item cover to an existing home policy or taking out specialist jewellery insurance, and either way you'll usually need a recent valuation or purchase receipt.
