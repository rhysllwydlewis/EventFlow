Event Travel Costs: Budgeting Transport for Guests, Staff and Suppliers

Travel is one of the easiest event costs to underestimate. Here's how to budget properly for guests, staff, stakeholders and suppliers getting to and from your event — and where the savings actually are.

The short version

  • Transport usually lands at 5–10% of total event spend, and it's typically the least itemised line on the sheet.
  • Guest travel is an attendance problem before it's a cost problem — a venue that's awkward to reach shows up in your RSVP numbers.
  • Supplier travel sits outside the headline day rate. Ask for the free-mileage radius before you compare quotes.
  • Reimburse staff at HMRC's approved rates and agree it in writing before anyone drives anywhere.
  • Price long or repeated journeys off real pump prices, not last quarter's estimate.
01

Why travel is the line that bites

Travel rarely gets its own line on an event budget — it gets absorbed into "miscellaneous" and then quietly becomes one of the biggest surprises on the final invoice. Once you add up parking, shuttle buses, taxis for VIPs, mileage for staff, and fuel or train fares for suppliers arriving from out of town, transport can easily account for 5–10% of total event spend, sometimes more for events spread across multiple sites or a full day of setup and breakdown.

It's also one of the few costs that scales with decisions you make elsewhere in the plan. Choose a venue an hour further from the motorway and every supplier's callout fee goes up. Add a second site for photos or an after-party and you've just doubled the transport logistics. Getting travel right early avoids both nasty budget shocks and, just as importantly, guests and crew who turn up stressed and late.

02

Getting guests to and from your event

Guest travel is about experience as much as cost. A venue that's difficult to reach — poor parking, no nearby station, unreliable evening buses — creates friction before anyone has even arrived, and it shows up in your RSVP numbers.

  • Parking: Confirm on-site capacity early. If it's limited, arrange overflow parking or a park-and-ride shuttle rather than leaving guests to find their own solution on the day.
  • Public transport links: Note the nearest station or stop, typical journey times, and last-service times in your invitations or event page — especially for evening finishes.
  • Shuttle buses: For venues off the beaten track, a shared shuttle from a central point (station, hotel, car park) is often cheaper per head than dozens of taxis, and it keeps arrivals on schedule.
  • Accessibility: Factor in drop-off points, step-free routes and accessible parking bays for guests with mobility needs — this affects venue choice as much as budget.
  • Accommodation-linked travel: If guests are travelling long distances, a block-booked hotel with a short transfer to the venue is usually easier to budget than dozens of individual journeys on the night.

Put a "Getting there" section on your invitation, wedding website or event page. Journey options, parking notes and last-train times cut down guest questions and reduce late arrivals — and it costs you nothing but ten minutes of writing.

03

Staff, stakeholders, suppliers and agencies

This is the travel spend that catches organisers out, because it's spread across several different budget lines rather than one obvious "guest transport" heading.

Suppliers and crew

Photographers, caterers, AV crews and entertainers often price travel separately from their core fee — either as a flat callout charge or mileage above a free radius (commonly the first 20–30 miles). Ask for this upfront when comparing quotes; a cheaper day rate with a large travel surcharge can end up costing more than a supplier based closer to the venue.

Staff and volunteers

For corporate events, staff travelling between office and venue — or between multiple sites during a multi-day conference — need a clear mileage or expenses policy agreed in advance, not worked out after the event when receipts start arriving.

Stakeholders and VIPs

Senior stakeholders, sponsors or speakers often warrant a more managed journey: a pre-booked taxi or a named parking space rather than being pointed to the overflow field. Budget for this separately so it doesn't get lost in the general transport line.

Agencies and multi-site activations

If you're running a touring activation, brand roadshow or multi-venue campaign, agency crews travelling with equipment between sites are usually your single largest transport cost. Build in fuel, van hire, congestion charges and overnight stays as a distinct budget block, and revisit it as the route firms up — early estimates based on a draft schedule are rarely accurate once real dates and distances are confirmed.

04

Calculating fuel and mileage costs

Most UK event organisers reimburse staff mileage using HMRC's approved rates — currently 45p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles in a tax year, dropping to 25p per mile after that, for cars and vans. Using the HMRC rate keeps reimbursements tax-free for the recipient and simple to administer, and it's a sensible default even for volunteers or freelance crew, provided everyone agrees to it in advance.

Where a flat mileage rate doesn't fit — a supplier quoting for a long return trip, a van hire for an activation tour, or a shuttle contractor pricing fuel separately — you'll need to estimate cost from actual distance and current pump prices rather than a rule-of-thumb figure. Fuel prices move enough week to week that an estimate from a few months ago can be noticeably out. Before you lock in a mileage rate or a fuel allowance for a big trip, it's worth checking current forecourt prices rather than guessing — a live tracker like fuelcosts.co.uk shows UK average petrol and diesel prices, which makes it much easier to set a realistic per-mile figure or price up a long-distance supplier journey accurately.

05

Ways to reduce event travel costs

  • Book local first: A supplier 10 miles away with a slightly higher day rate is often cheaper overall than one 60 miles away once travel and time are added.
  • Share the journey: Group crew and equipment into fewer vehicles rather than everyone driving separately — it also simplifies parking and arrival logistics.
  • Time it around traffic: Scheduling setup or breakdown outside peak hours can meaningfully cut both journey time and fuel use for repeat trips.
  • Negotiate a travel-inclusive rate: For suppliers making several visits (planning meetings, setup, event day), agree one travel figure upfront instead of paying mileage per trip.
  • Use a shared shuttle instead of individual taxis: One minibus running a fixed route is almost always cheaper per person than several taxis booked separately.
  • Check the free-mileage radius: Many suppliers quote a free travel zone — confirm the exact mileage rather than assuming, since "local" means different things to different businesses.
06

Building travel into your event budget

Treat travel as its own budget category rather than folding it into venue or supplier costs — it makes it far easier to see where the money is actually going and to compare quotes fairly. As a starting point:

  1. List every group that needs to travel: guests, staff, suppliers, stakeholders, agency crew.
  2. Note distance, likely mode of transport and any surcharge or free-mileage radius for each.
  3. Add a contingency of around 10% for fuel price movement, route changes or last-minute additions.
  4. Review actual costs after the event so next time's estimate starts from real numbers, not guesswork.
07

Common questions

Should you pay for guests' travel to an event?

For most weddings, parties and public events, guests cover their own travel. What organisers usually fund is the part guests can't arrange themselves — a shuttle from the station or hotel, overflow parking, or accessible drop-off. For corporate events where attendance is expected rather than optional, budget for travel and accommodation as part of the invitation.

What mileage rate should you pay event staff and volunteers?

HMRC's approved mileage allowance payments are 45p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles in a tax year and 25p per mile after that, for cars and vans. Paying at or below those rates keeps the reimbursement free of tax and National Insurance. Agree the rate in writing before anyone drives, including with volunteers and freelance crew.

Do event suppliers include travel in their quote?

Not consistently. Many suppliers include travel within a free radius — commonly 20 to 30 miles — and charge mileage or a flat callout fee beyond it. Others price travel separately for every visit. Ask for the free-mileage radius and the rate beyond it before you compare day rates, or a cheaper headline quote can end up costing more.

How much of an event budget should go on transport?

Transport typically absorbs 5–10% of total event spend, rising for multi-site events, touring activations and venues far from motorways or stations. Give travel its own budget category rather than folding it into venue or supplier lines, and add around 10% contingency for fuel price movement and route changes.
