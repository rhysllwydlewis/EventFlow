Wedding Planner vs DIY Planning Guide

What a wedding planner costs and does, DIY planning pros and cons, and hybrid options like partial planning or day-of coordination.

The short version

  • Wedding planners come in three main tiers: full planning, partial planning, and day-of coordination — each a very different scope and price.
  • Full planning is often quoted around 10–15% of the total wedding budget, or as a flat fee; day-of coordination is far cheaper.
  • DIY planning saves the planner's fee but takes a genuine time commitment and someone willing to handle problems on the day itself.
  • Day-of coordination is a popular middle ground: plan everything yourself, then hand the wedding day itself to a professional.

What a Wedding Planner Does

"Wedding planner" covers a range of different services, and the right tier depends on how much of the process you want to hand off.

  • Full planning: involved from concept through to execution — budget management, sourcing and booking suppliers, design, logistics and running the day itself.
  • Partial planning: the couple books some or all suppliers themselves, then the planner joins a few months out to pull everything together, fill gaps and manage the final run-up.
  • Day-of (on-the-day) coordination: the couple plans and books everything; the planner takes over purely to run the wedding day itself — timeline, supplier briefing and troubleshooting.

A venue's own in-house coordinator (where one is included) usually only manages the venue's own logistics on the day, not the couple's full supplier list — worth clarifying early, since it's a common source of confusion about what's already covered.

Wedding Planner Costs

UK wedding planner fees vary considerably by region, experience and scope. Full planning is often quoted as a percentage of the total wedding budget, commonly cited around 10–15%, or alternatively as a flat fee. Partial planning generally costs less than full planning but more than day-of coordination. Day-of coordination, covering only the wedding day itself, is far cheaper and often a few hundred pounds. For how planner fees compare to the rest of a wedding budget, see the UK Wedding Cost Guide 2026 and EventFlow's wedding budgeting guide.

Get a clear written scope of what's included before booking — the same job title covers very different levels of involvement between planners, so compare like for like rather than headline price alone. EventFlow's event contracts and deposits guide covers what to check in any supplier agreement.

DIY Planning: Pros & Cons

Pros

  • No planner fee, freeing up budget for suppliers, the venue or the honeymoon.
  • Full control over every decision and supplier choice, with no one else's schedule to work around.
  • A more personal, hands-on process for couples who enjoy the planning itself.

Cons

  • A genuine time commitment — researching, contacting and comparing suppliers, and tracking a growing list of tasks and deadlines.
  • The couple (or family) usually has to handle problems on the day itself, at the moment they'd rather be enjoying it.
  • Less access to a planner's existing supplier relationships and negotiating experience.

EventFlow's own event planning checklist and wedding DIY projects guide are both built to support a DIY approach directly.

Hybrid Options

Day-of coordination is the most common hybrid: the couple plans and books everything themselves using tools like EventFlow's checklist and budget planner, then hands the wedding day itself to a professional coordinator so nobody in the wedding party has to manage suppliers, timing or problems on the day.

Asking a trusted friend or family member to act as an informal "day-of contact" is a free alternative, though it comes without a professional coordinator's experience handling supplier logistics and unexpected issues — and it takes that person out of properly enjoying the day as a guest.

Common questions

Do I need a wedding planner?

No wedding needs a planner, but whether you'd benefit from one depends on how much time you have, how many suppliers you're coordinating, and how much stress you're comfortable taking on yourself. A full-time job, a long guest list or a venue that doesn't include an in-house coordinator are all common reasons couples hire help.

How much does a wedding planner cost in the UK?

UK wedding planner fees vary considerably by region and scope. Full planning is often quoted as a percentage of the total wedding budget, commonly cited around 10–15%, or as a flat fee. Day-of coordination, which covers only the wedding day itself, is far cheaper and often a few hundred pounds.

What is day-of coordination?

Day-of (or 'on-the-day') coordination means a planner takes over running the wedding day itself — managing the timeline, briefing suppliers and handling anything that goes wrong — while the couple does all the planning beforehand. It's a popular middle ground between full DIY and hiring a full-service planner.
