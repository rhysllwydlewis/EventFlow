Wedding Cake Guide

How to choose a wedding cake, from style and flavour to sizing, costs and ordering timeline.

The short version

  • Most UK wedding cakes cost £200–£600, rising with tier count, design detail and dietary/allergen requirements.
  • Book a baker 4–6 months ahead, and 9–12 months for a popular baker or a peak season Saturday.
  • Rule of thumb: each tier of a standard round cake serves roughly double the tier above it.
  • Cutting the cake is a nice-to-have, not a must — dessert tables, doughnut walls and cheese-tower "cakes" are all established UK alternatives.

Choosing a Cake Style & Flavour

A wedding cake is one of the few suppliers that also feeds every guest, so style and flavour both matter — and they don't need to match the theme of the room to work well together.

Style

  • Traditional fondant-covered: the classic smooth, tiered look, well suited to intricate piping or sugar flowers.
  • Naked or semi-naked: sponge with little or no outer icing, exposed layers visible. Popular for rustic and outdoor weddings, and typically cheaper than full fondant work.
  • Buttercream: a softer, textured finish — palette-knife or "watercolour" styles are a current favourite.
  • Textured/trend finishes: pressed flowers, dried fruit, metallic leaf or piped florals as an alternative to smooth fondant.

Flavour

Many bakers offer a different flavour per tier — a classic vanilla or Victoria sponge alongside something bolder like lemon, red velvet or chocolate biscuit. A tasting session, usually included in the baker's price, is the best way to decide, and it's the moment to flag any dietary requirements (see EventFlow's RSVP management guide for collecting these from guests generally).

Sizing & Servings

Cake size is driven by guest count, not by how the cake looks in photos — though the two can be reconciled with a simple trick.

Ask your baker about "dummy" tiers — polystyrene or cake-drum layers iced to match the real tiers, with the servable cake cut from a sheet cake in the kitchen. It's a common way to get a tall, dramatic cake photo without paying for slices that go to waste.

Costs & Ordering Timeline

Most UK wedding cakes cost £200–£600. Where a cake lands in that range depends on tier count, the complexity of the design and any dietary adaptations — gluten-free, vegan and allergen-free cakes are widely available but often cost more per serving. For how cake spend compares to the rest of a wedding budget, see the UK Wedding Cost Guide 2026.

Timeline

  • 9–12 months before: book a popular baker, especially for a peak-season Saturday.
  • 4–6 months before: book if you haven't already — this is the minimum for most bakers.
  • 2–4 months before: attend a tasting and agree flavours, size and rough design.
  • 6–8 weeks before: confirm final guest numbers, exact design details and delivery/setup time with the venue.

Check the cancellation and postponement terms before paying a deposit — cakes are made to order and can't be resold if plans change. EventFlow's event contracts and deposits guide covers what to check before booking any supplier.

Cutting the Cake & Alternatives

Cutting the cake is a well-known moment in the reception running order, but it's optional — and a growing number of UK couples skip or replace it.

Cake-cutting traditions

  • The couple cuts the first slice together, traditionally the bottom (largest) tier
  • Freezing the top tier for the first anniversary is a long-standing tradition, though less common now that cakes are smaller
  • Some couples ask the caterer to serve slices as dessert rather than staging a separate cutting moment

Alternatives to a traditional tiered cake

  • Dessert table: a spread of smaller bakes — cupcakes, tarts, brownies — instead of one cake
  • Doughnut wall: a popular, informal alternative that doubles as decor
  • Cheese "cake": tiers built from wheels of cheese, popular for savoury-leaning receptions
  • Cupcake tower: easier to serve without a knife and naturally portion-controlled

Any of these can still include one small cutting cake on top for photos, so the tradition and the practical serving don't have to be the same object.

Common questions

How far in advance should I order my wedding cake?

Book a wedding cake baker 4 to 6 months ahead, and 9 to 12 months for a popular baker or a peak season (May–September) Saturday. A tasting usually happens a few months out, with final flavour, size and design details confirmed 6 to 8 weeks before the wedding.

How much does a UK wedding cake cost?

Most UK wedding cakes cost between £200 and £600, depending on the number of tiers, design detail and whether it needs to cater for allergies or dietary requirements. A simple two-tier cake sits at the lower end; an elaborate multi-tier design with sugar flowers or intricate icing work costs more.

What size cake do I need for my guest count?

As a rough guide, a two-tier cake serves around 50–75 guests, a three-tier around 75–120, and a four-tier 120–150 or more. Ask your baker about dummy (display-only) tiers cut from a sheet cake in the kitchen — they keep the tall, dramatic look for photos while only the tiers that need to feed everyone are "real".
