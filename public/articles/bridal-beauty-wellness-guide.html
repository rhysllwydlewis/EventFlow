Bridal Beauty & Wellness Countdown

A month-by-month bridal beauty and wellness timeline for brides, covering skincare routines, hair and makeup trials, the final weeks and wedding-day self-care.

The short version

  • Start any new skincare routine at least 3 months before the wedding — never trial a new product or treatment in the final few weeks.
  • Book hair and makeup trials 2–3 months out, ideally alongside a dress fitting so you can see the full look together.
  • Avoid dramatic changes — a new fringe, a big tan change, strong new actives — in the final 2 weeks.
  • A small on-the-day touch-up kit handed to a bridesmaid prevents most last-minute panics.

6–12 Months Out

The earlier a skincare or treatment plan starts, the less risk there is of a reaction or breakout showing up right before the wedding — this is the window for anything that needs real lead time.

  • New skincare routines: start early. Stronger actives like retinol or chemical peels can cause an initial "purge" before skin improves, so they need months, not weeks, to settle.
  • Dental care: book any whitening or dental work well ahead — teeth whitening especially can leave temporary sensitivity.
  • Facial courses: a course of facials over several months gives more visible results than a single treatment the week before.

The golden rule of wedding beauty: nothing new in the final few weeks. Save any product or treatment you haven't tried before for well ahead of the big day.

Hair & Makeup Trials

A trial is where the look actually comes together — it's worth treating as seriously as a dress fitting, not an afterthought.

  • Timing: book 2–3 months before the wedding, on a day you can see the result in daylight and take proper photos to compare later.
  • Bring the details: inspiration photos, your veil or hairpiece, and any accessories, so the trial reflects what you'll actually wear on the day.
  • Test it properly: if possible, wear the trial look to a hen do or another event — a few hours out is a better test than a quick mirror check.

The Final Weeks

  • No new products: avoid trying anything untested in the final 2 weeks — a reaction now is much harder to fix than one caught months earlier.
  • Fake tan: patch-test weeks in advance, then book a trial tan a week or so before the wedding, followed by the final application 1–2 days before.
  • Nails: book a manicure or gel set 1–2 days before, giving enough time for any smudges to be fixed without rushing on the morning itself.
  • Rest and hydration: sleep and water intake affect skin more in the final week than any product — an early night matters more than a last-minute treatment.

On the Day

The morning of the wedding is not the time to discover the timeline is too tight — build it backwards from the ceremony time with your hair and makeup artist.

  • Buffer time: ask your artist how long hair and makeup will realistically take for the full bridal party, then add a buffer for delays.
  • Eat and hydrate: a rushed morning often means skipped meals — have breakfast and keep water on hand throughout getting ready.
  • Touch-up kit: blotting papers, a lipstick or lip colour, safety pins, tissues, pain relief and a small mirror covers most last-minute issues. Hand it to a bridesmaid or your wedding coordinator so it's on hand without you having to think about it.

Common questions

When should I start a bridal skincare routine?

Start any new skincare routine at least 3 months before the wedding, and ideally 6–12 months out if you're planning stronger treatments like chemical peels or retinol, which need time to settle and can cause purging. Never trial a brand-new product or treatment in the final few weeks.

How far in advance should I book hair and makeup trials?

Book hair and makeup trials 2 to 3 months before the wedding, ideally on a day you can see the result in daylight and photograph it properly. Bring inspiration photos and any accessories, such as a veil or hairpiece, so the trial matches what you'll actually wear.

What should be in a wedding-day emergency beauty kit?

A small kit covers most last-minute issues: blotting papers, a lipstick or lip colour for touch-ups, safety pins, tissues, pain relief and a small mirror. Hand it to a bridesmaid or the wedding coordinator so it's on hand without you having to think about it.
