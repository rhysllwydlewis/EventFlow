2. Personal Data We Collect

We collect and process different types of personal data depending on how you interact with our Services:

2.1 User Account Data

When you create an account with EventFlow, we collect:

  • Identity Data: Your name, email address
  • Account Credentials: Password (stored as a cryptographic hash using bcrypt), user ID
  • Account Information: User role (customer, supplier, or admin), verification status, account creation date
  • Usage Data: Last login date, marketing preferences, email verification status
  • Profile Data: User preferences, settings, saved content

2.2 Supplier Account Data

If you register as a supplier, we additionally collect:

  • Business Information: Supplier name, business description, contact details
  • Location Data: Business or venue address and postcode, derived base city or coordinates, service areas, travel radius and geographic coverage
  • Commercial Data: Packages offered, pricing information, availability
  • Performance Metrics: Reviews, ratings, health scores, approval status
  • Professional Status: Pro membership status, verification badges

2.3 Customer Planning Data

When you use EventFlow to plan events, we collect:

  • Event Plans: Event details, budgets, timelines, notes
  • Guest Information: Guest lists, invitation details (where provided)
  • Communication Data: Messages sent through our platform, conversation threads
  • Preference Data: Saved suppliers, comparisons, shortlists

2.4 Photos and Media

We collect and store:

  • Uploaded Images: Photos uploaded by suppliers or users
  • Media Metadata: Upload date, file information, associated supplier
  • Moderation Status: Approval status, rejection reasons, admin notes

2.5 Technical and Usage Data

We automatically collect certain information when you use our Services:

  • Device Information: IP address, browser type and version, device type
  • Usage Information: Pages visited, features used, time spent on the platform
  • Authentication Tokens: JWT tokens stored in HTTP-only cookies for session management

2.6 Fraud and Abuse Prevention Data

For Partner Programme and supplier registration security, we may process technical signals to detect fake accounts, self-referrals, automated registrations and coordinated abuse.

  • Pseudonymous Network Signals: HMAC-protected representations of IP addresses and network ranges used for short-term correlation and registration velocity checks.
  • Pseudonymous Browser Signals: Browser type, language and standard browser client hints combined into a protected signature. This is a risk signal and is not treated as proof that two people are the same person.
  • Email Integrity Signals: Protected email identity and domain representations used to detect known disposable email services and common alias-reuse patterns.
  • Optional Network Reputation: Where configured, a specialist provider may assess an IP address for proxy, VPN, Tor, hosting/datacentre or abuse-reputation indicators. Shared networks or ordinary VPN use are not, by themselves, treated as proof of fraud.
  • Decision Evidence: Risk scores, reason codes, administrative review notes and temporary overrides used to explain and audit anti-abuse decisions.

Partner anti-abuse technical event evidence is normally retained for up to 90 days (unless a different documented operational period is configured). Appeals are normally retained for up to 180 days so that we can investigate and respond. We provide a review/appeal route where automated controls block a registration.

2.7 Marketplace Data

When you use the EventFlow Marketplace to list, browse, or purchase event items, we collect:

  • Listing Information: Item title, description, price, location, category, condition, availability status
  • Item Photos: Images you upload for marketplace listings (stored in dedicated MongoDB collections alongside the listing records)
  • Transaction Data: Listing status (active, sold, removed, expired), view counts, listing creation and modification dates
  • Communication Data: Messages exchanged between buyers and sellers through our platform messaging system
  • User Activity: Items viewed, searches performed, saved listings, listing performance metrics

Important: EventFlow does not collect or process payment information for marketplace transactions. Buyers and sellers arrange payment directly using their chosen payment methods (PayPal, bank transfer, cash, etc.). We are not party to marketplace transactions and do not handle money transfers.

2.8 Community Data

When you use the EventFlow Community, we collect and derive:

  • Public Profile Data: A public handle, display name and avatar, plus any optional biography, broad UK region or event type you choose to provide
  • Public Content: Discussions, replies, tags, reactions, polls and their edit and activity dates
  • Personal Organisation Data: Drafts, bookmarks, follows, muted items and Community notification preferences
  • Safety and Moderation Data: Reports, report details, content snapshots, moderation actions and reasons, restrictions, appeals, risk signals and audit records
  • Community Statistics: Post and reply counts, helpful reactions, upheld reports, contribution levels and other service-integrity measures
  • View De-duplication: For signed-in members, a user identifier; for other visitors, a short pseudonymous value derived from IP address and user-agent. The underlying IP address and user-agent are not stored in the Community view record, and the de-duplication record expires after 24 hours

Published Community profiles, discussions and replies are public and may be found through EventFlow search or indexed by external search engines. Community posts accept only a broad region and month-level event date; they do not collect an exact venue, address or postcode as structured Community fields. Members should still avoid placing personal or confidential information in free-text content.

5. Data Storage and Security

5.1 Storage Infrastructure

The application is designed to use a configured MongoDB deployment for persistent production data:

  • Application Data: Accounts, listings, messages and service records are stored in MongoDB.
  • Uploaded Media: Processed supplier, profile and marketplace images are stored in MongoDB collections.
  • Hosting Region: The operator selects the production provider and region. The live region must be confirmed against the deployment configuration and provider agreement.

5.2 Security Measures

We implement comprehensive technical and organizational security measures:

Authentication Security:

  • JSON Web Token (JWT) based authentication
  • HTTP-only, secure cookies to prevent XSS attacks
  • Password hashing using bcrypt with appropriate salt rounds
  • Minimum 32-character JWT secret keys
  • Rate limiting on authentication endpoints to prevent brute force attacks

Authorization and Access Control:

  • Role-based access control (RBAC) system
  • Separation of privileges between customers, suppliers, and administrators
  • Owner account protection preventing unauthorized changes
  • Admin-only restrictions on sensitive operations

Network Security:

  • HTTPS encryption (TLS/SSL) for all data in transit in production environments
  • Cross-Site Request Forgery (CSRF) protection middleware
  • Secure headers and content security policies
  • Regular security updates and patches

Data Protection:

  • Passwords never stored in plain text
  • Sensitive data excluded from data exports
  • Provider encryption and backup controls where enabled for the live deployment

5.3 Payment Security

Subscription & Service Payments:

  • Payment Processor: Stripe
  • Data Handling: EventFlow does NOT store or process payment card details directly
  • Platform Records: We store Stripe customer, subscription, checkout and invoice references needed to administer the subscription, rather than full card details
  • Transmission: Payment information is sent to Stripe over an encrypted connection
  • Authentication: Stripe may apply 3D Secure or Strong Customer Authentication where required for a transaction

Marketplace Transactions (Peer-to-Peer):

  • Payment Processing: EventFlow does NOT currently process marketplace payments
  • Arrangement: Payment arrangements made directly between buyers and sellers
  • No Financial Data: We do not collect or store payment card details for marketplace transactions
  • Transaction Records: We only store transaction metadata (listing ID, communication logs) not financial details
  • User Responsibility: Users responsible for choosing secure payment methods

Future Payment Processing:

  • If EventFlow implements integrated marketplace payment processing, it will be via PCI DSS Level 1 certified processors
  • Users will be notified of any changes to payment processing methods
  • This privacy notice will be updated to reflect any new payment data handling

7. Third-Party Services

We use third-party services to operate EventFlow. Depending on the service and context, a provider may act as our processor or as an independent controller under its own privacy information. Only providers enabled in the live deployment process data.

7.1 Email Delivery

  • Postmark: Transactional email delivery for verification, password resets, service notifications and support messages. Marketing messages are sent only where the applicable permission or other lawful basis exists.

Data involved: Email address, name, message content and delivery metadata. In local development, email may instead be written to a local outbox and is not sent to Postmark.

7.2 Database and Uploaded Media

  • MongoDB: The production database stores application records and processed image data. The specific MongoDB hosting arrangement and region depend on the live deployment.

Data involved: User and supplier records, messages, Community and marketplace records, audit information, and processed supplier, profile and marketplace images.

7.3 Payments and Sign-in

  • Stripe: Processes supplier subscription checkout, billing and payment events. EventFlow receives the account and transaction references needed to manage the subscription.
  • Google Identity Services: If you choose Google sign-in, Google provides a verified account identifier and profile information such as email address, name and profile image. Google also receives information needed to complete the sign-in request.
  • Sign in with Apple: If you choose Apple sign-in, Apple provides a verified account identifier and, on your first sign-in only, your name and an email address (which may be an Apple-generated private relay address if you chose to hide your email). Apple also receives information needed to complete the sign-in request.
  • Facebook Login: If you choose to continue with Facebook, Facebook provides a verified account identifier and profile information such as your email address and name. Facebook also receives information needed to complete the sign-in request.

7.4 Location and Media Services

  • Postcodes.io: We may send a supplier or venue postcode to the Postcodes.io service to derive coordinates and a city mapping. We use that result for supplier search, service-area matching and UK location pages.
  • Pexels: We use Pexels to source and deliver licensed imagery, including images for UK city pages. When an image is loaded from a Pexels domain, Pexels may receive ordinary web-request data such as IP address, browser information, the requested image URL and referring page.

City-page searches sent to Pexels contain a city or region description, not the visitor's search text or account details. Please see the relevant provider's privacy information for its own processing.

7.5 Analytics and Service Reliability

  • PostHog: If you consent to analytics, PostHog may process privacy-minimised page, journey, attribution, conversion and performance events. If enabled, it may also provide masked session replay on non-sensitive pages. Inputs are masked, captured URLs have query strings removed, and replay is blocked on sensitive areas such as messages, payments, dashboards and settings.
  • Sentry: Where configured, Sentry processes error reports and sampled performance traces so that we can diagnose faults and protect the reliability and security of the Services. Authentication cookies and authorisation headers are removed from server error events before they are sent.

Data involved: page path without query strings, event and performance data, acquisition source, opaque session or user identifiers, device/browser details and diagnostic error context. The providers may receive an IP address as part of an ordinary network request. We do not intentionally send passwords, form values or message content to PostHog. Sentry error context can vary with the fault, so access is restricted and reports should not be used to submit sensitive information.

11. Data Breach Notification

In the event of a data breach that poses a risk to your rights and freedoms, we will:

  1. Contain the breach: Take immediate action to identify and stop the breach
  2. Assess the impact: Determine the scope, severity, and affected data
  3. Notify authorities: Report to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) within 72 hours if required
  4. Notify affected users: Inform you without undue delay if the breach poses a high risk to you
  5. Document the breach: Maintain records of the breach and our response
  6. Implement safeguards: Take corrective action to prevent future breaches

If we notify you of a data breach, we will provide information about what happened, what data was affected, and what steps you should take to protect yourself.
